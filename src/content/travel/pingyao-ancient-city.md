Step through the gates of **Pingyao** (平遥古城) and you enter a time capsule. This [UNESCO World Heritage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingyao) in Shanxi Province is China's best-preserved walled city, with its Ming Dynasty walls, Qing Dynasty shop fronts, and medieval street plan virtually unchanged since the 14th century.

## The Walls That Saved a City

Pingyao's rectangular city wall, built in 1370, stretches 6.4 kilometers with 72 watchtowers and 3,000 battlements. The numbers are symbolic — 72 for the 72 sages of Confucius, 3,000 for the 3,000 disciples. The walls average 12 meters high and 5 meters thick, with rammed-earth cores faced in brick.

Unlike most Chinese city walls demolished during modernization, Pingyao's survived because the county was too poor to tear them down. Poverty became preservation — a fortunate irony that saved one of [China's greatest architectural treasures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingyao_City_Wall).

## China's First Bank

Pingyao's most significant historical contribution is financial. In 1823, a local merchant named Lei Lutai established **Rishengchang** (日升昌) — China's first draft bank (*piaohao*, 票号). Instead of transporting silver across dangerous roads, merchants could deposit silver in Pingyao and withdraw it at any Rishengchang branch using a paper draft.

At its peak, Pingyao's piaohao network controlled over half of China's silver circulation. The city was the Wall Street of [Qing Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) China — a remarkable legacy for a small Shanxi county town.

## Walking the Old Streets

Pingyao's grid street plan follows classical Chinese urban design: a central north-south axis with cross streets forming a chessboard pattern. The main **Ming-Qing Street** (明清街) is lined with traditional shop fronts selling Pingyao beef, aged vinegar, and lacquerware.

The Rishengchang museum, housed in the original bank building, shows how draft banking worked — secret codes embedded in poetry, invisible ink, and elaborate anti-counterfeiting measures centuries before modern cryptography.

## Beyond the Walls

The nearby **Shuanglin Temple** contains over 2,000 painted clay sculptures from the Song through Ming dynasties — some of the finest Buddhist art in China. The **Zhenguo Temple** pagoda, built in 963 CE, is one of China's oldest wooden structures.
