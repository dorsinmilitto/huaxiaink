When night falls over **Fenghuang** (凤凰古城), thousands of red lanterns reflect off the Tuojiang River, transforming this 1,300-year-old town into a scene from a Chinese ink painting. It is, by common consensus, China's most beautiful riverside ancient town.

## A Town Built on Stilts

Fenghuang's defining feature is its **diaojiaolou** (吊脚楼) — wooden houses perched on stilts over the river. Built by the Miao people, these structures date back to the [Ming Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) and remain inhabited today. The stilts allow the river to flow beneath the houses during flood season, while providing cool ventilation in summer.

The architectural logic is brilliant: no foundation digging needed on rocky riverbanks, natural flood protection, and a raised living space above damp ground. Walking along the river at dusk, you see lamplight glowing through wooden shutters while water laps at the pilings below.

## The Miao and Tujia People

Fenghuang sits in [Xiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangxi), western Hunan — home to the Miao and Tujia ethnic minorities. The town was a military garrison on the Miao frontier during the Ming and Qing dynasties, and ethnic culture remains vibrantly alive.

Women in elaborate silver jewelry and indigo-dyed batik clothing are common sights. The Miao silver — necklaces, headdresses, and bracelets weighing several kilograms — is both adornment and wealth storage, passed from mother to daughter across generations.

## The Night River Walk

The Tuojiang River walk is Fenghuang's signature experience. Stone-paved paths follow both banks, crossing the river via the **Hongqiao** (Rainbow Bridge) and the **Tiaojing** stepping-stone bridge. At night, the water becomes a mirror of red and gold lantern light.

Bar streets and tea houses line the river. Musicians play the *bawu* (a Miao bamboo flute) from upstairs windows. The atmosphere is somewhere between Venice and a Chinese scroll painting — and entirely its own thing.

## Beyond the Tourist Core

The surrounding countryside offers [hiking trails](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H6GWV4YD?tag=huaxiaink-20) through terraced rice paddies and Miao villages. The nearby **Nanfang Great Wall** — a Ming-era wall built to separate Miao territory from Han lands — is a stark reminder of the region's frontier history. Unlike the [Great Wall near Beijing](/en/travel/great-wall-beyond-badaling/), this wall was specifically for ethnic boundary enforcement.
